CERAMICS / TOKONAME

Tokoname and surrounding areas, Aichi

Tokoname: Making Room for a Teapot

You do not need a complete tea service. A small teapot and cups you already own are enough to add a pause to the day. Start exploring Tokoname by asking how many people you make tea for, rather than how much you know about tea ceremony.

2026.11.08 / KOTO

This regional example is separate from the contemporary maker discussed below.

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Begin with your own senses

Look beyond color to the handle, spout and lid. Will you pour into two small cups or make one large mug? A comfortable empty pot may feel different when full. If you can handle a sample, consider the movement of lifting and pouring, not only the resting shape.

02 / MAKERS TODAY

JINSUI / TOKI

JINSUI's TOKI teapots connect Tokoname craft with time spent enjoying tea. The official range includes KINARI, SHIROHAI and SUMIKURO. It is a useful starting point if your image of the region has been limited to a red-clay pot.

03 / AT YOUR TABLE

Three ideas for your table

The following are KOTO editorial suggestions.

01

Morning tea

Keep the pot and cup together on a small tray, with a convenient place to store them.

02

Tea for two

Cups can differ in color while having similar capacities, making portions easier to judge.

03

Something sweet

Pair the pot with a plain plate in a related tone and let the confection add color.

Before you choose

Check whether the listed capacity is brimful or practical, and inspect the strainer design. Follow the maker's cleaning instructions. The historical jar shown here illustrates Tokoname's background; it is not a photograph of a TOKI teapot.

04 / EXPLORE THE REGION

Connect with the region

The cooperative provides routes to clay, glaze and maker information. It is perfectly reasonable to find a pot you like first and learn about its production afterward. In a shop, explain how many servings you make and which leaves you use; those details can guide a useful conversation.

とこなめ焼協同組合 / Tokoname pottery cooperative ↗
Regional background and sources

Tokoname is known for red-clay teapots, whose color comes from iron in the clay. Its production also includes glazed vessels and flower containers. Tea utensils offer a starting point for exploring different surfaces and forms.
