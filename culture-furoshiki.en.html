CULTURE / CLOTH AND GIFTS

Furoshiki: Choosing Cloth, Thinking of a Gift

Before tying the knot, spread out a cloth. Discover the pleasure of pairing a furoshiki with a gift, through colour, pattern and a little everyday history.

2026.11.09 / KOTO

01 / BEFORE THE KNOT

A little time before wrapping

Set a box of sweets on the table, then spread a wrapping cloth beside it. The pleasure of choosing a gift can last a little longer here. Perhaps a quiet blue, perhaps a soft red. Think of a colour your friend often wears, or the hallway you will step into when you visit.

A furoshiki is a roughly square cloth used to wrap and carry things. Its appearance changes around an object: a straight edge emerges along a box, while folds gather near the knot. Part of the pleasure is watching a flat piece of fabric take on a temporary shape.

02 / A CLOTH IN DAILY LIFE

Everyday life behind the name

Fukuoka City Museum explains that square wrapping cloths were once commonly called hirazutsumi, and came to be known as furoshiki during the Edo period. The name is associated with cloth used to carry bathing supplies and for other purposes at the baths. The connection with bathing offers a glimpse of daily life.

The museum also describes large cloths made to carry bedding and household belongings when a woman married. Wrapping cloth belonged both to ordinary journeys and to important transitions. That range of uses gives a familiar object another dimension.

03 / PATTERNS TO LOOK AT

A pattern, open and folded

One example on Japan’s Cultural Heritage Online is a furoshiki with noshi and takarazukushi, or assorted treasure motifs, held by the ICU Hachiro Yuasa Memorial Museum. Dated to the Meiji–Taishō period, it is a bast-fibre cloth decorated using tsutsugaki, measuring about 140 by 128 centimetres. The museum’s description identifies takarazukushi as an auspicious motif associated with good fortune.

A historic cloth can prompt a fresh look at one you might use today. Which part of a large design will appear on top? How will a smaller pattern run through the folds? Turn the cloth beneath a box and notice how the balance of colours changes.

04 / FINDING A PAIR

A quiet conversation with the gift

Try a deep-coloured cloth around a bright confectionery box. A book with a pale cover might invite a similarly gentle shade. Even when the wrapping hides the object, the pairing you considered remains a small, private pleasure.

Then notice the fabric itself: its surface, its weight, the way it catches the light. Something that seemed restrained on a shelf may feel surprisingly lively in your hands. You need not learn every pattern’s meaning before finding a cloth that feels right for this particular gift.

These colour pairings are KOTO’s suggestions for enjoyment, rather than rules of formal etiquette. For a ceremonial occasion, a knowledgeable shop can help you choose something appropriate to the recipient and purpose.

05 / WHEN THE CLOTH OPENS

After the knot is undone

For an informal gift to a friend, a few words can make clear whether the cloth is part of the present or simply there for carrying it. If you are giving both, share why you chose the fabric: “I thought you would like this blue.” A small observation can make the wrapping feel personal.

Once untied, the bundle becomes a flat cloth again. Wrapped around something else, it will reveal a different arrangement of the same pattern. Choosing furoshiki offers time to think about another person, and to imagine a combination you have not seen yet.
