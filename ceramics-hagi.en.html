CERAMICS / HAGI

Hagi and surrounding areas, Yamaguchi

Hagi: Soft Colors for an Afternoon Pause

A brief tea break can still include the pleasure of choosing a cup. Hagi's soft character can belong on a small table at home as readily as in a formal setting. Begin with the moment you want to make space for.

2026.11.08 / KOTO

This regional example is separate from the contemporary maker discussed below.

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Begin with your own senses

Color names do not tell the whole story. Notice clay showing through a glaze, thicker pools of glaze and the line of a rim. Similar-looking pieces can behave differently in changing light. Also consider whether you enjoy a surface that evolves or prefer a simpler care routine.

02 / MAKERS TODAY

松光山 / Shokouzan

Shokouzan, based in Oi in Hagi, presents vessels for everyday food and flowers. Its official shop includes coffee ware, offering an introduction beyond the tea bowl. Start by looking for a comfortable handle and a capacity that suits what you regularly drink.

03 / AT YOUR TABLE

Three ideas for your table

The following are KOTO editorial suggestions.

01

Afternoon tea

Pair a pale vessel with plain cloth and one sweet, keeping the palette quiet.

02

Breakfast yogurt

Add fruit and a wooden spoon to a small bowl without replacing the rest of your tableware.

03

Coffee for two

Similar capacities and heights can unite cups from different series.

Before you choose

Absorbency and changes with use vary by piece. Ask whether a vessel suits your intended liquids and how to prepare and dry it. Follow the maker's instructions rather than trying to accelerate changes in its surface.

04 / EXPLORE THE REGION

Connect with the region

The cooperative introduces a region that includes both tea-ceremony work and everyday tableware. Compare several practices rather than treating one kiln as representative of everything. Use current exhibition and opening information when planning a visit.

萩焼協同組合 / Hagi pottery cooperative ↗
Regional background and sources

Hagi ware is associated with a soft clay character and the tea ceremony. Its absorbency can cause colors to change with use. Ask the maker how a particular piece may change and how it should be cared for.
