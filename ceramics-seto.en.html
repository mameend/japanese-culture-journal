CERAMICS / SETO

Seto, Aichi

Seto: Blue and White for Bread and Coffee

Blue-and-white pottery need not be reserved for Japanese food. Bread or cheese on a painted plate can make an ordinary table feel fresh. White and blue offer an accessible way into Seto's broad ceramic landscape.

2026.11.08 / KOTO

This regional example is separate from the contemporary maker discussed below.

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Begin with your own senses

Notice the thickness of a line and whether a picture sits in the center or around the edge. Food can reveal a pattern, frame it or gradually uncover it. Think of serving as making the final composition, rather than trying to preserve an empty plate's appearance.

02 / MAKERS TODAY

眞窯・加藤真雪 / Singama and Mayuki Kato

Singama makes painted blue-and-white vessels in Seto. Its official site introduces fourth-generation maker Mayuki Kato and FLOW, a collaboration with designer Takumi Shimamura. Look at how a painted flower interacts with the object's shape: the relationship between surface and form is part of the design.

03 / AT YOUR TABLE

Three ideas for your table

The following are KOTO editorial suggestions.

01

Bread and cheese

Set a small portion on a blue-patterned plate and add clear glassware.

02

Afternoon cake

Place a slice slightly off-center to reveal part of the decoration.

03

Everyday dinner

Add one painted small bowl to a plain white main plate, keeping blue as a single accent.

Before you choose

Picture a new piece beside any blue ceramics you own. Similar shapes or amounts of undecorated space can unite different blues. The horse-eye plate pictured here is a historical example, separate from Singama's current products.

04 / EXPLORE THE REGION

Connect with the region

The cooperative's member directory offers a route to other Seto makers. Compare neighboring practices after finding a brand you like. Treat retail visits and workshop visits as separate arrangements, checking access rather than assuming a production space is open to the public.

愛知県陶磁器工業協同組合 / Aichi ceramics industry cooperative ↗
Regional background and sources

Seto produces a broad range of ceramics, from tableware to decorative objects. Akazu ware and Seto Sometsuke ware are also made within the region. Think of Seto as a place containing many techniques and kinds of work.
