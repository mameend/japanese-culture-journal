CERAMICS / TSUBOYA

Tsuboya in Naha, Yomitan and other areas, Okinawa

Tsuboya: Bring a Travel Memory to the Everyday Table

A vessel you fall for while travelling can remain part of life at home. Imagine your usual meals alongside your memories of Okinawa. Using the piece is one way to continue the journey.

2026.11.08 / KOTO

Photography is being prepared. Explore current works through the official links in this article.

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Begin with your own senses

Consider what you would enjoy serving, alongside line and color. Leave some pattern visible or pair the piece with a plain dish. You do not need a special recipe to explore its character.

02 / MAKERS TODAY

育陶園・Kamany / Ikutouen’s Kamany

Ikutouen describes Kamany as a brand begun in 2011 by younger makers exploring Tsuboya ware suited to their own lives. Explore that connection between tradition and daily use through its official photographs, alongside the studio’s account of materials and making.

03 / AT YOUR TABLE

Three ideas for your table

The following are KOTO editorial suggestions.

01

Morning toast

Pair a patterned plate with a plain mug and an ordinary breakfast.

02

Vegetable supper

Repeat one color from the vessel in cloth or a chopstick rest.

03

Start small

Use a side plate for fruit or a snack before committing to larger pieces.

Before you choose

Before taking a piece home, confirm its intended food use and care. See the official site for current work.

04 / EXPLORE THE REGION

Connect with the region

The cooperative lists members and events. Research a few workshops before travelling, then inspect weight and the underside when you see a piece in person. Enjoy individual practices rather than treating one kiln as representative of all Okinawan ceramics.

壺屋陶器事業協同組合 / Tsuboya pottery cooperative ↗
Regional background and sources

Rooted in Tsuboya in Naha, this tradition is now also practiced beyond that district. It includes arayachi, associated with storage jars, and glazed joyachi, which encompasses bowls, plates and other everyday vessels.
