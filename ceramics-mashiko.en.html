CERAMICS / MASHIKO

Mashiko, Tochigi

Mashiko: An Everyday Plate for a Slow Breakfast

Swap the usual breakfast plate for one with a different rim or glaze. The toast stays the same, but the meal feels newly considered. You can begin exploring Mashiko with something you want to use tomorrow, without first learning a vocabulary of ceramics.

2026.11.08 / KOTO

This regional example is separate from the contemporary maker discussed below.

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Begin with your own senses

Rather than looking for one supposedly typical brown glaze, study the outline and usable surface. A wide rim leaves space around toast; a little depth helps contain salad. Weight also matters differently in a cup you lift and a plate that stays on the table.

02 / MAKERS TODAY

よしざわ窯 / Yoshizawa Gama

Yoshizawa Gama's official shop presents oval and rectangular plates alongside seasonal motifs. These shapes invite you to imagine a meal. Compare the usable area as well as the decoration, so that your favorite piece becomes part of breakfast rather than something that only sits on a shelf.

03 / AT YOUR TABLE

Three ideas for your table

The following are KOTO editorial suggestions.

01

Breakfast toast

Put toast and an egg on an oval plate; a plain cup or clear glass lets the outline stand out.

02

A bakery treat

Place one pastry slightly off-center on a small plate, leaving some of its pattern visible.

03

Lunch for one

Use a shallow bowl for salad, with bread alongside. Keep wet dressings or sides in a separate dish.

Before you choose

Start with one plate. Measure your shelf and think about the bread you usually eat. Check dishwasher and microwave guidance for the particular product, even when you already own another piece from the same maker.

04 / EXPLORE THE REGION

Connect with the region

Follow your first maker with a look at the Mashiko Pottery Cooperative. It offers a route from individual objects to the organization behind the region. Before visiting, shortlist a few pieces and confirm where they are sold and when the shop is accessible.

益子焼協同組合 / Mashiko Pottery Cooperative ↗
Regional background and sources

Mashiko developed through everyday vessels such as storage jars and grinding bowls. Its connections with Shoji Hamada and the mingei movement offer another way into its ceramics. Begin by comparing the forms and the character of the clay.
