# A Single Flower, a Small Place at Home

Before learning a flower’s name, notice the curve of its stem and the direction of its leaves. A single flower and a small vessel can be a way into looking at ikebana.

01 / BEGIN WITH LOOKING


## A moment before finding its place



You come home from the florist with one flower. Look beyond the petals to the way its stem bends and its leaves extend. Turn it slightly from the angle you first thought of as the front. A line that was hidden a moment ago may come into view.



Once the flower is in a vessel, take a few steps back. Then look from the chair where you usually sit. Move between the small details you notice up close and the way the flower sits within the room. Begin by making a little time for that kind of looking.



02 / TWO WAYS OF SEEING


## Words that help us look



Ikenobo’s official introduction describes finding beauty in a bud as well as in an open flower. It connects our attention to plants with a sense of time passing. Read Ikenobo’s introduction



Sogetsu’s official introduction emphasizes the individuality and free expression of the person arranging. Read About Sogetsu



Return to your flower after reading these two introductions. Alongside wondering what shape to make, try asking what caught your attention. Each school has its own ideas and ways of learning. Let a phrase that interests you be a starting point for exploring its work, without treating these approaches as a single set of rules.



03 / AT HOME


## Three small experiments with a flower and a vessel



These are KOTO’s suggestions for enjoying flowers at home, rather than formal instruction in an ikebana school’s techniques.

01

### Try another angle


Choose a flower vessel that holds water and stands securely. Change the flower’s direction a little at a time. When you find a line you like, pause there.

02

### Clear a little background


Move one nearby object to leave some space behind the flower. Before adding anything, notice how the outlines of the stem and leaves look.

03

### Look back at the vessel


Let your eye travel from the flower to the opening of the vessel. Look at the two together and explore a combination that feels comfortable to you.



04 / A SMALL DAILY PAUSE


## Tomorrow, from the same chair



Before your morning tea, look at the flower from yesterday’s chair. Remember just one thing that catches your eye: the appearance of a bud or leaf, perhaps, or the way light falls. Add a little time for looking to the pleasure of having flowers at home.



Follow the seller’s advice on changing the water and caring for the stems of your particular flowers. Choose a stable position away from busy paths through the room. If children or pets share your home, check whether a flower is suitable before buying it.



If you would like to learn more, explore the work and lesson information on the schools’ official websites. Take your own small observation with you: “I like this line.” It may help you find something of your own in the next arrangement you see.
