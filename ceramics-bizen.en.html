CERAMICS / BIZEN

Bizen and surrounding areas, Okayama

Bizen: Earthy Surfaces, Everyday Meals

If a brown vessel seems too austere, imagine white tofu or green vegetables on it. A restrained surface can make the food's color and shape more noticeable. Approach Bizen through tonight's meal rather than through knowledge of antiques.

2026.11.08 / KOTO

This regional example is separate from the contemporary maker discussed below.

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Begin with your own senses

Compare where light and dark areas fall on two similarly shaped plates. Where would you place the food? How might the surface look in the light at home? These are ways to enjoy firing marks even before learning their specialist names.

02 / MAKERS TODAY

一陽窯 / Ichiyougama

Ichiyougama in Inbe is listed by the local tourism association. Okayama's official tourism guide also describes its kiln, workspace and hand-building experience. It is a candidate for exploring production as well as finished objects; confirm current access and booking conditions before making plans.

03 / AT YOUR TABLE

Three ideas for your table

The following are KOTO editorial suggestions.

01

Tofu

Leave some earthy surface visible around white tofu and a small garnish.

02

A green side

Put greens or beans in a small bowl and balance the setting with a white plate.

03

Coffee and a treat

Use a Bizen side plate with a cup you already own; changing one item is enough.

Before you choose

Inspect the rim and the foot that meets the table. Follow piece-specific guidance, including drying after washing. The historical sake bottle pictured here is not a photograph of an Ichiyougama work.

04 / EXPLORE THE REGION

Connect with the region

The association's official shop offers another route into the region's work. Choose a category—plates, cups or vases—before comparing pieces. Focus on what suits your hands and eyes rather than expecting a vessel to produce a particular change in a drink's flavor.

Regional background and sources

Bizen is fired without applied glaze or painted decoration. Ash, flame and placement in the kiln contribute to its surfaces. Even vessels of similar shape invite close comparison of their colors and markings.
