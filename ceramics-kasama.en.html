CERAMICS / KASAMA

Kasama, Ibaraki

Kasama: Finding Your Own Rhythm in Clay

Your tableware does not need to match. A smooth cup beside a bowl with fine ridges can be a pleasure in itself. That contrast is a useful starting point for Kasama, where following individual makers is often more rewarding than searching for one regional look.

2026.11.08 / KOTO

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Begin with your own senses

Look at where light collects in a photograph. Ridges create shadows, while a broad surface gives the glaze room to show. A side view can help you imagine the rim and where your fingers would rest. These observations are useful when choosing online, too.

02 / MAKERS TODAY

額賀章夫 / Akio Nukaga

Kasama City's profile identifies Akio Nukaga's pleated shinogi work and sabi-kohiki surfaces. Looking at the repeated lines as you might look at pleated fabric provides an approachable connection between clothing and ceramics. The official profile's photographs are a good place to compare rhythm and texture.

03 / AT YOUR TABLE

Three ideas for your table

The following are KOTO editorial suggestions.

01

Breakfast for one

Put yogurt and fruit in a textured bowl; plain cutlery and a simple tray keep the surface in focus.

02

Tea with friends

Different cups can work together when the cloth beneath them shares one color.

03

An evening side

Pair one earthy small bowl with a plain white plate, using beans or vegetables as a modest side dish.

Before you choose

Prioritize how a vessel fits your hands over the reputation of its maker. Look for dimensions, weight and the maker's description. Explore current work by Akio Nukaga through the official link above.

04 / EXPLORE THE REGION

Connect with the region

The cooperative introduces the region and the Himatsuri pottery festival. Use it to find names to follow, then compare two or three makers rather than trying to see everything. Check the current official festival notice before planning around dates or exhibitors.

笠間焼協同組合 / Kasamayaki Cooperative ↗
Regional background and sources

Kasama's production has expanded from kitchen vessels to tableware and flower vessels. Rather than looking for one defining color or pattern, look for the forms and expressions of individual makers.
